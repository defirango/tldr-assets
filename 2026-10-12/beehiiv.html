News that matters

Ledger's CryptoBilis drain: up to $93M traced, $10M frozen

Ledger's 72-hour update on 11 October says every confirmed case from the 9 October drain involves devices sold by the Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis, and that its own systems and direct sales channels are unaffected. Ledger has confirmed an unauthorized hardware implant in at least one device. Bitquery traces $93.2M from 315 wallets across six chains, and YFarmX puts it at $94.5M from 521 addresses, per CoinDesk. Tether froze $10M of USDT. Mark Karpelès says the implant board carries a microcontroller, an antenna, an LTE chip and an e-SIM, and reads what the screen shows. The tampered units pass Ledger's Genuine Check. CryptoBilis's founder says the company was sold to an undisclosed buyer in March. Separately, X user Johannes posted photos of an implant in a Ledger he says he bought in store from a European reseller. Reports name MediaMarkt. Ledger has not commented on Europe and the claim is unverified.

Why it matters. The drain ran through a reseller Ledger listed as authorized, and Ledger's own software check passed the tampered units. Ledger has not said whether it will reimburse anyone.

If you hold or use this: Bought from CryptoBilis: do not set up unused devices, and move funds to a new Ledger-bought device with a new seed. Bought elsewhere from a reseller: Genuine Check alone will not catch this.

Sources: Ledger on X, CoinDesk, Bitquery, Help Net Security, Johannes on X, Bitcoin.com: Europe report, Bitcoin.com: CryptoBilis founder

One thing to learn

Supply-chain attack. In a hardware-wallet supply-chain attack the device is modified before it reaches you, so the seed leaks the moment you set it up. In the CryptoBilis case the security chip was real, which is why Ledger's Genuine Check passed. Buying direct from the maker removes the reseller layer where the implant went in.
