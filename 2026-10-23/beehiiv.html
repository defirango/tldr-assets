Ethereum delays Glamsterdam on Hoodi

The Ethereum Foundation postponed the 26 Oct Glamsterdam activation on the Hoodi testnet and the planned 200M gas limit raise, per Crypto Times. ChainSecurity found that gas changes in EIP-8037 and EIP-8038 can break existing contracts and says it already has 13 real-world examples.

Why it matters. Some operations could restore gas and let a contract's failure-handling code keep running, which breaks apps that retry failed calls based on gas left. Others could cost more than existing gas limits allow. No new date is set, and mainnet users have nothing to do.

If you hold or use this: Running contracts on Ethereum: ChainSecurity asks teams to check them now.

Sources: ChainSecurity on X, Crypto Times, ChainSecurity report

One thing to learn

Infinite-mint exploits. On a chain with its own tokens and bridges, a bug in the minting logic lets an attacker create tokens with no backing. The attacker then bridges or swaps them out for real assets. The bridge sees a valid transfer and pays out from its reserves even though the mint was never authorized. Low-TVL chains are most exposed, because the bridge is often the deepest pool of liquidity (Grok).
