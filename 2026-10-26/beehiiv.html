Strive buys $511M of BTC as Strategy pauses

Strive bought 6,182 BTC for about $511.4M between 17 and 23 Oct, at an average of $82,732 each, per its SEC filing. Holdings rose to 43,000 BTC from 36,818. It also opened a $1B at-the-market program for its 15% SATS preferred stock.

Why it matters. Strategy went the other way. It bought no BTC last week, ending a five-week streak, and spent $204.9M buying back STRC preferred from its cash, per DailyCoin. It holds 848,517 BTC. Strive's coins cost $3.8B against a $3.5B fair value, per its prospectus supplement as reported by The Crypto Times. CEO Matt Cole's post drew 172K views.

If you hold or use this: Holding ASST or SATS: the $1B ATM can add up to 10M SATS shares, and every new share carries a 15% dividend (The Crypto Times).

Keystone leaks buyer data and its device key

Keystone said an attacker used a flaw in a third-party monitoring tool on its website to grab 25,964 buyer records with names, contacts and shipping addresses, plus 12,873 unfinished checkouts. The attacker also took the key Keystone uses to check that devices are genuine. Seeds, private keys and signing were not touched, per Keystone.

Why it matters. The key sits in devices from the factory and cannot be changed, so a counterfeit using it could pass Keystone's checks, per The Crypto Times. Keystone will destroy 18,835 devices in stock and paused web orders until a new key is ready. Its 180-day deletion rule relied on a bulk-delete tool that failed.

If you hold or use this: Own a Keystone: delivered before 20 Oct, no action needed. Delivered later, verify it with support. Expect phishing and recall scams at your address.

Sources: Keystone on X, The Crypto Times

Electrum bug turned fee bumps into theft

Every Electrum version before 4.8.3 trusted its server for pending transactions without checking signatures. A malicious server could swap in a fake transaction paying an attacker, and a user who bumped its fee would sign a real one, per the Electrum advisory reported by crypto.news. At least one theft is confirmed. The amount is undisclosed.

Why it matters. Electrum shipped 4.8.3 on 24 Oct and said some servers were already exploiting the flaw, per CoinDesk. Its release post has 117K views. Users connected only to servers they trust, including their own, were not exposed.

If you hold or use this: Using Electrum: upgrade to 4.8.3 from the official site now. Windows Defender may flag ZXing.dll, which Electrum calls a false positive.

Sources: Electrum on X, Electrum advisory, CoinDesk, crypto.news

CFTC pushes a voluntary path for crypto exchanges

CFTC Chairman Mike Selig said the agency is advancing a voluntary federal registration path for crypto exchanges while Congress has yet to act. Exchanges that opt in could offer retail leveraged or margined crypto trading under federal rules, per crypto.news.

Why it matters. The CLARITY Act failed a Senate procedural vote 49 to 50 on 15 Sep. The CFTC proposal opened for comment on 5 Oct and comments close on 14 Dec, so nothing is in force yet, per crypto.news. It trended on X's news tab.

Sources: Mike Selig on X, crypto.news

Upbit and Bithumb flag INIT after the Initia hack

Upbit and Bithumb put INIT on trading caution at 14:00 KST on 26 Oct, per Blockchain Reporter. Upbit says the cause of the exploit is not identified or resolved, and INIT deposits stay blocked. Initia's chain has been halted since 23 Oct after about $2.7M was bridged out (Grok).

Why it matters. Upbit reviews the designation from 23 to 27 Nov, then extends it, lifts it or ends INIT trading. Initia says a fix will be done and audited within days. The full post-mortem is still pending, per Blockchain Reporter.

If you hold or use this: Holding bridged assets on Initia: avoid new deposits until the post-mortem (Grok).

Sources: Blockchain Reporter, DefiLlama hacks

One thing to learn

Funding rates. On perpetual futures, a positive funding rate means longs pay shorts to keep their positions open. If price stalls while funding stays positive, holding the long keeps costing money, and that pressure can trigger long liquidations even without a big spot selloff (Grok).
