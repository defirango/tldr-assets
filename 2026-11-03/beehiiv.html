News that matters

Binance traces the $6.3B hack to a fake job interview

Binance published its initial report on the 29 Oct incident at 14:33 UTC. In September, a Binance software engineer was approached on a job platform by someone posing as a project director. After a video interview with a fake HR rep and a chat interview with a fake CEO, he downloaded a coding "assignment" onto his work computer. It carried 0-day malware that swapped out a pyenv component installed through Homebrew, and Binance's endpoint tools missed it. On 20 Oct the attacker used that laptop and an internal AI tool to take over two wallet-team engineers, one of whom held a container deployment token. On 29 Oct that access reached the wallet signing service.

Why it matters. About $6.34B was hit and $2.85B has been frozen or intercepted. Binance says 98% of the impacted token assets were replenished by 31 Oct and it will absorb the loss itself. Pausing withdrawals could not stop the drain on its own: the attacker had set up EIP-7702 delegations on-chain, and the hot wallet used MPC, so there was no single key to revoke them fast. The last transfer, 2,961.9 ETH, settled at 23:56 UTC on 29 Oct. Binance says it blocked 57 suspected North Korea-linked attempts in 2026 and has rebuilt the systems in the attack path. The report post drew 3.6M views, the biggest crypto post of the window.

If you hold or use this: Binance user: check the latest withdrawal update for your network. Builder or job hunter: treat any recruiter's coding test as hostile code.

Sources: Binance incident report on X, Binance blog, BleepingComputer, Binance withdrawal update on X, Watcher.Guru on X

North Korea denies the Binance hack

Kim Yo Jong, Kim Jong Un's sister, called US claims linking Pyongyang to the Binance theft "absurd rhetoric", Reuters reported citing state agency KCNA (CryptoSlate). The FBI's 31 Oct alert pinned the theft on TraderTraitor, its name for a North Korean state hacking group, and asked exchanges and DeFi services to block the listed addresses.

Why it matters. Binance's report cites the FBI's finding that North Korea is responsible. TRM Labs also attributes the hack to TraderTraitor with high confidence and says most funds moved within about 20 minutes from 20:05 UTC on 29 Oct (CryptoSlate). Watcher.Guru's post on the denial drew 1.13M views.

Sources: Watcher.Guru on X, CryptoSlate

CFTC clears perps on single US stocks

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig said perpetual futures on individual stocks can now trade on CFTC-registered exchanges that are also notice-registered with the SEC as securities exchanges. They count as security futures under the Commodity Futures Modernization Act of 2000, with joint CFTC and SEC oversight under an MOU the two agencies signed in March. No venues or launch stocks were named (CoinDesk).

Why it matters. Selig says the aim is to give US traders a regulated home for a product they now use on offshore venues. It follows the CFTC's 29 May approval of a bitcoin perp on a US exchange. Perps did more than $630B in volume in the past month (DefiLlama via CoinDesk). The CFTC's post drew 320K views and Watcher.Guru's 392K.

Sources: CFTC on X, CFTC statement, CoinDesk

Liquid's LBTC is fully backed again

The Liquid Federation said at 00:03 UTC that the sidechain's bitcoin reserves are fully restored, so every LBTC is backed 1:1 again. The 6 Sept exploit minted about 4,000 unbacked LBTC and cut the reserve from about 4,205 BTC to 197. The attacker returned 3,400 BTC the next day, leaving a gap of about 600 BTC (CoinDesk).

Why it matters. The federation has not said who supplied the missing BTC. BTC and LBTC have swapped 1:1 through providers such as SideSwap since late October. Direct federation withdrawals still run under enhanced controls, and governance changes are promised in the coming weeks (CoinDesk, Liquid Federation).

If you hold or use this: Holding LBTC: check the reserve on liquid.network or verify it from your own Liquid node.

Sources: Liquid Federation on X, CoinDesk, Blockstream incident report

Mercado Bitcoin: $38M hit, $11M blocked

Brazil's largest exchange said late on 2 Nov that about $38M in assets were involved in Monday's incident and that $11M has been blocked with help from Tether, Circle, Paxos, BRL1 and Chainalysis. It says customers will not lose any assets and their balances remain recorded.

Why it matters. Deposits and withdrawals are still paused with no restart date, and the exchange says it has notified the authorities. More amounts that can be blocked have been identified.

If you hold or use this: Mercado Bitcoin user: wait for the restart notice on the official channels. There is no date yet.

Sources: Mercado Bitcoin on X, Mercado Bitcoin update on X

New Jersey drops its Coinbase staking case

New Jersey's Bureau of Securities withdrew its 2023 cease-and-desist order against Coinbase's staking service, along with the $5M penalty. The notice is dated 30 Oct and the withdrawal is without prejudice, so the state could bring a new case later (Cointelegraph).

Why it matters. Coinbase said at 15:48 UTC that New Jersey users can stake again. California, Maryland, Wisconsin and Washington still have cases open (Cointelegraph). Watcher.Guru's post drew 758K views.

Sources: Watcher.Guru on X, Cointelegraph, Coinbase on X

One thing to learn

EIP-7702. EIP-7702 lets an Ethereum account hand some execution rights to a smart contract without exposing its private key. In the Binance attack, the attacker used stolen signing access to grant its own contract partial rights over a hot wallet. Those authorizations lived on-chain, so they kept working after Binance paused withdrawals. The hot wallet used MPC, so there was no single key to revoke them quickly. Binance cleared the delegation at 00:10 UTC on 30 Oct (Binance).
