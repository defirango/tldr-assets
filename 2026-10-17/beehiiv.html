Nine firms form a Quantum Alliance

Quantus, Postquant Labs, Midnight Foundation, Keystone, Abelian, Hacken, The Vault, American Fortress and Intmax launched the Quantum Alliance on 15 Oct. It runs as an open standards body modeled on the IETF. It will publish an open definition of quantum readiness, tests any team can run on its own code and reference designs for migration. Work starts in four groups: migration, post-quantum ZK, wallet security and cross-chain compatibility. The Alliance counts 6.04M BTC, 30.2% of supply, as exposed. In the same week Google said its Willow chip cut errors about 40-fold when preparing magic states, a key input for large quantum programs.

Why it matters. CoinDesk says Google's result does not make a Bitcoin attack 40 times cheaper. Project Eleven's Alex Pruden said it validates existing Q-day estimates, per The Block. Postquant Labs' Richard Carback sees 2028 as a realistic date and prefers moving vulnerable coins to freezing them.

If you hold or use this: Holding BTC in an address that has already spent: its public key is public for good. Watch for migration guidance.

Sources: Hacken on X, Decrypt, CoinDesk, Crypto Briefing, Google, CoinDesk on Willow, The Block

Deutsche Bank BTC custody is weeks away

Deutsche Bank's crypto custody service is weeks from launch, corporate bank co-head Gerald Podobnik told Reuters on 16 Oct, per Bitcoin Magazine. It starts in Germany for institutional and corporate clients, and the bank holds the wallets and keys. Bitcoin and ether come first. The September release also lists USDC, EURC and EURAU. Moving money into and out of stablecoins comes later, and the bank plans to take custody to Asia next year.

Why it matters. Germany's largest lender joins BNY Mellon, State Street, Standard Chartered, U.S. Bank and Citi in offering or committing to direct crypto custody, per Bitcoin Magazine. Go-live still depends on the regulatory timeline.

Sources: Bitcoin Magazine, Deutsche Bank, The Cryptonomist

Schwab opens crypto trading to all clients

CEO Rick Wurster said on Schwab's Q3 call that all clients can now access Schwab Crypto, closing a phased rollout that began in May. It offers spot BTC and ETH through Charles Schwab Premier Bank, with Paxos handling sub-custody and execution. The fee is 0.75% of each trade with no spread. New York and Louisiana are excluded, and accounts still go through review.

Why it matters. Bitcoin Magazine's post on the rollout had 90.3K views when we read it. Schwab plans three more coins by year-end, and it named SOL, AVAX and LINK in August. Holdings sit outside SIPC and FDIC cover, per FinanceFeeds.

If you hold or use this: Buying through Schwab: compare the 0.75% fee with what you pay elsewhere.

Sources: Bitcoin Magazine on X, FinanceFeeds

Pirate Chain drops its rollback plan

A validation bug let an attacker mint about 39.8M ARRR, about 20% of supply, straight into the Sapling shielded pool. Pirate Chain first said it would roll back to 14 Oct. It now says a rollback or a network freeze is impossible because the chain is decentralized. dPoW notaries paused notarizations, exchanges and pools were asked to pause, and NonKYC restricted the account behind suspicious deposits. The team sees no sign that notary keys or the shielded cryptography were compromised.

Why it matters. Shielded transfers mean the minted coins cannot be fully traced. Recovery steps are due in a post-mortem. ARRR trades at $0.2412, down 3.2% in 24h on CoinGecko.

If you hold or use this: Holding ARRR: keep funds still until the post-mortem lands.

Sources: Pirate Chain on X, The Crypto Times

Coinbase to power Marqeta stablecoin cards

Marqeta is expanding its six-year Coinbase partnership so businesses can issue cards funded by customers' stablecoins. Coinbase holds the stablecoins and handles conversion, Marqeta issues the cards and processes payments, and settlement runs on Base.

Why it matters. Card programs can be funded 24/7. Fees, launch date and the first supported stablecoins are not public yet.

Sources: The Block

One thing to learn

Exposed public keys. Most Bitcoin addresses hide the public key behind a hash until the owner spends, and some older formats show it from the start. After the first spend, the key sits onchain for good. Classical computers cannot turn it back into the private key. A large enough quantum computer could, and none exists yet. That is why the Alliance counts 6.04M BTC as exposed and wants tests that prove quantum-resistance claims.
