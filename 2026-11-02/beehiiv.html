News that matters

Binance users can now verify 1:1 backing

Binance's Recovery Update #5 at 12:49 UTC says the full zero-knowledge proof for its 31 Oct reserves snapshot is done. Anyone holding BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL, USDC, XRP, USD1, RLUSD or ZEC on Binance can check that their balance is backed at least 1:1. About 95% of the hacked amount is covered. The last 5% is less liquid tokens outside the top ten that take longer to buy at size.

Why it matters. The next phase of withdrawals for more networks starts tomorrow as a progressive rollout, and a new snapshot covering more tokens is set for 4 Nov, with its proof taking 40 to 50 hours (Binance). In the first 9.5 hours after withdrawals reopened on 1 Nov, Binance processed $4.649B out and $1.01B in, a net $3.64B outflow. DefiLlama's 24h net outflow figure has since fallen to about $3.29B from a peak near $7B (The Block). OKX took in about $450M net and Bybit about $134M (CoinDesk). Binance's first-day withdrawal post drew 1M views and Update #5 drew 461K.

If you hold or use this: Holding one of the ten tokens on Binance: run the verifier from the PoR site. Waiting on another network: the rollout starts 3 Nov.

Sources: Binance Recovery Update #5 on X, Binance on first-day withdrawals, The Block, CoinDesk live updates, SecurityWeek on the FBI attribution

Mercado Bitcoin pauses withdrawals after $40M+ drain

Brazil's largest crypto exchange said it "identified and swiftly contained" a security incident early Monday and suspended deposits and withdrawals as a precaution. Security firms put the losses above $40M, drained from wallets on at least 10 chains including Ethereum, Solana, Tron, Polygon, Arbitrum, Avalanche, BSC and Stellar. The largest outflows ran from 06:19 to 06:53 UTC (CoinDesk). Arkham data puts the total at about $46.3M (The Block).

Why it matters. The exchange says customers were not harmed and no data leaked, and it published the destination wallets so trackers can follow the funds. Tether froze more than 4.5M USDT tied to the incident. The transfers were plain transactions with no smart contract bug, and how the attacker got signing access is still unknown. Nobody has been blamed, and there is no public evidence linking it to the Binance hack (CoinDesk). Watcher.Guru's post on the hack drew 1.1M views, the biggest crypto post of the window.

If you hold or use this: Using Mercado Bitcoin: wait for the official restart of deposits and withdrawals, and follow @MercadoBitcoin for updates.

Sources: Mercado Bitcoin statement on X, Mercado Bitcoin destination wallets on X, Paolo Ardoino on the USDT freeze, CoinDesk, The Block

Strategy adds 811 BTC as treasuries keep buying

Strategy bought 811 BTC and repurchased $208M of its STRC preferred stock. It now holds 849,327 BTC and $4.9B of USD assets (Michael Saylor). Strive added 124 BTC for $10M at an average $80,526 and now holds 43,124 BTC. Metaplanet bought back about 96.5M shares in the second half of October for ¥25.3B, about $160M (CoinDesk).

Why it matters. The buying comes after a weak October for funds. US bitcoin ETFs lost $515M in the month, ending a two-month inflow streak. Ether ETFs saw $1.61B of net outflows, their largest month since launch, with BlackRock's ETHA losing $1.42B (SoSoValue via CoinDesk). Saylor's post drew 396K views.

Sources: Michael Saylor on X, CoinDesk live updates

One thing to learn

SAFU. SAFU is Binance's Secure Asset Fund for Users, a reserve held largely in BTC that pays for user losses in a breach. Tapping it means the exchange takes the hit itself. Repeated large events test how deep the fund really is (Grok).
