News that matters

Binance says 90% of stolen funds are replenished

Binance's Update #3 at 14:30 UTC says coverage rose from about 80% to 90% of the roughly $6.3B taken. BTC, ETH, USDT, BNB, SOL, USDC, XRP, USD1, RLUSD and ZEC are fully backed. The money came from a partial SAFU deployment, the excess in the Futures insurance fund and Binance's corporate treasury. Binance later said the SAFU transfer was a single 7,499 BTC move. Withdrawals remain on track to reopen Sunday, 1 Nov.

Why it matters. The last 10% is less liquid tokens outside the top ten, which Binance is still buying on market and OTC. A new Proof of Reserves snapshot was taken at 00:00 UTC on 31 Oct, and the full zero-knowledge proof should be ready by 3 Nov. Binance says it will refill SAFU to about $1B. Update #3 drew 825K views. Update #2, which first put coverage at 80% and set a phased restart, drew 1.27M.

If you hold or use this: Holding one of the ten fully backed tokens on Binance: check your 1:1 backing yourself once the ZK proof is out. Expect withdrawals to reopen in phases.

Sources: Binance Update #3 on X, Binance Update #2 on X, Binance on the SAFU transfer, Business Today

Trump says he is looking into the hack as calls for rules grow

At a Friday press conference, Trump said he was looking into the Binance hack and that Binance says customers will get their funds back. "But it's a hack," he added. CFTC Chair Michael Selig told CNBC that Binance is not federally regulated by his agency and called the real issue "unregulated offshore exchanges" (The Block).

Why it matters. Sen. Cynthia Lummis said the stalled Clarity Act would have let crypto firms freeze suspicious transactions when law enforcement asks. The Digital Chamber wrote to House and Senate leaders urging a bill that gives the CFTC authority over crypto exchanges before this Congress adjourns. Bitcoin Archive's clip of Trump drew 154K views and Watcher.Guru's post 451K.

If you hold or use this: Following policy: the Clarity Act is the bill to watch. It passed the House last year and has stalled in the Senate (The Block).

Sources: Bitcoin Archive on X, The Block, Watcher.Guru on X

ICE deletes, then reposts its Hyperliquid perps plan

ICE deleted its X post on bringing Hyperliquid-based perps to the US with Trade[XYZ] a day after announcing the deal. Its press release stayed up (Bloomingbit, via Odaily). At 11:35 UTC ICE posted the plan again. The new version says ICE "plans to" launch subject to regulatory approvals, with US traders coming in through regulated FCMs, including ICE's joint venture with OKX.

Why it matters. HYPE started dumping when the post vanished, per Ted (@TedPillows), and was down 4.5% in 24h on CoinGecko at 16:51 UTC after a 16% gain on the week. Eleanor Terrett said the legal team was "probably just being extra cautious". The repost drew 211K views.

If you hold or use this: Holding HYPE: the venue still needs US regulators to approve it before any launch.

Sources: ICE on X, Eleanor Terrett on X, Ted on X, Bloomingbit

18,270 stolen BTC sit untouched in 19 wallets

The BTC taken from Binance, about $1.4B, left a hot wallet in 11 transactions to one address, then split across 19 addresses: 17 holding 1,000 BTC each plus about 833 and 438 BTC. None had been spent at CoinDesk's last check. The stash is bigger than Tesla's 11,509 BTC and about 2.3 times El Salvador's reserve.

Why it matters. The Block shows how the attacker beat stablecoin freezes. It swapped $325M of stablecoins into $285M of ETH in about 20 minutes and posted $59.9M of USDT on Aave to borrow 19,762 ETH. About $628M also moved through TRON, per TRM Labs as cited by Coinpaper. TRX fell 7.3% in 24h as its hack-linked rally reversed.

If you hold or use this: Holding BTC: an 18,270 BTC overhang sits in known wallets. The first move out of them is the signal to watch.

Sources: CoinDesk, The Block, Coinpaper

A dozen DeFi sites hijacked through Name.com domains

Blockaid flagged 12 DeFi sites rigged with Step Drainer, all registered with Name.com, including Flap, Kintsu, Pickle Finance, Origami Finance, FluxBeam, Echelon, StonkFun and StakePoint. By 12:32 UTC on 31 Oct, 10 were suspended at the registrar. Flap and Kintsu still resolved at publication. The Block traced about $138,000 stolen to three EVM wallets, before counting Solana and Aptos.

Why it matters. The sites use different DNS providers, so the registrar is the common link. Kintsu and Origami blamed Vercel, which had not commented. Step Drainer is sold as malware-as-a-service for about $750.

If you hold or use this: Using any of these dapps: do not connect a wallet until the team confirms the site is clean.

Sources: The Block

Chainalysis says an AI-assisted hacker got customer names

Chainalysis said a hacker using "offensive agentic techniques" broke into its systems and internal tools in October and accessed user IDs, full names, emails and role labels. It found no sign of access to investigation data, passwords, MFA secrets or session tokens (The Block).

Why it matters. Chainalysis says the breach is unrelated to the Binance theft. It has not said how many customers were affected.

If you hold or use this: Chainalysis client: turn on 2FA and watch for unexpected password resets or login prompts.

Sources: The Block

One thing to learn

Proof of Reserves. Proof of Reserves is an exchange showing it holds at least as much of each token as users are owed. Binance took a new snapshot at 00:00 UTC on 31 Oct. Its zero-knowledge proof takes 40 to 50 hours to generate and lets each user check that their own balance is backed 1:1 without seeing anyone else's. It covers user assets. Binance reports its Futures insurance fund separately (Binance).
